## Our Commitment

I want everyone who participates in my projects and communities to have
a welcoming, respectful, and positive experience.\
Whether you are contributing code, opening an issue, reviewing a pull
request, sharing an idea, reporting a bug, asking a question, or simply
following the project, you are expected to treat other with respect.

These projects are build by people with different backgrounds,
experiences, opinions, and levels of knowledge. Good collaboration
depends on creating en environment where people can learn, contribute,
disagree, and improve together.

By participating in these projects, you agree to follow this
**Code of Conduct**.

<!-- # expected behavior ------------------------------------------------- # -->

## Expected Behavior

I expect everyone participating in the project to:

- Treat other people with respect and professionalism.
- Communicate clearly, constructively, and in good faith.
- Be open to different perspectives and approaches.
- Give constructive feedback without attacking or insulting others.
- Assume good intentions while remaining open to correcting
  misunderstandings.
- Respect different levels of experience and knowledge.
- Help keep discussions focused on the project and its goals.
- Accept reasonable criticism and corrections.
- Give credit to other people's work and contributions.
- Respect the privacy and boundaries of other contributors.
- Use project resources responsibly.
- Help create an environment where people feel comfortable asking
  questions and learning.

You do not need to agree with everyone.

Healthy disagreement is a normal and valuable part of software
development. What matters is how that disagreement is communicated
and handled.

<!-- # unacceptable behavior --------------------------------------------- # -->

## Unacceptable Behavior

The following behavior is not acceptable:

- Harassment, discrimination, intimidation, or personal attacks.
- Threats of violence or any form of threatening behavior.
- Deliberate humiliation, bullying, or hostile behavior toward others.
- Hate speech or derogatory comments targeting individuals or groups.
- Sexual harassment or unwanted sexual attention.
- Sharing someone's private or personal information without permission.
- Doxxing or attempts to identify or expose private information.
- Deliberate disruption of project discussions, repositories, or
  community spaces.
- Trolling or intentionally provoking other participants.
- Repeatedly ignoring reasonable requests from maintainers or moderators.
- Impersonating contributors, maintainers, or other individuals.
- Maliciously misrepresenting another person's work or statements.
- Any other behavior that could reasonably make the project unsafe,
  hostile, or unwelcoming.

Disagreements about technical decisions, project direction,
implementation choices, or opinions are not inherently violations
of this **Code of Conduct**.

The focus should be on the behavior and its impact, not on preventing
legitimate technical disagreement.

<!-- # communication ----------------------------------------------------- # -->

## Communication

Good communication is an important part of open-source development.\
When discussing technical subjects, focus on the problem, the proposed
solution, and the available evidence rather than the person
presenting an idea.

It is okay to say that something is wrong.\
It is not okay to attack someone because they made a mistake or
disagree with you.

Written communication can also be easy to misunderstand. Before assuming
bad intentions, consider whether a clarification may resolve
the situation.

At the same time, repeated or clearly harmful behavior should not be
excused simply because it happens through text or online communication.

<!-- # constructive disagreement ----------------------------------------- # -->

## Constructive Disagreement

Different opinions and approaches are expected.\
Contributors may disagree about:

- Architecture.
- Code style.
- APIs.
- Dependencies.
- Documentation.
- Product decisions.
- Project priorities.
- Performance.
- Development workflows.

These discussions are welcome when they remain respectful and focused on
the subject.\
A strong technical argument should be evaluated based on its reasoning,
evidence, and impact on the project—not on who made it.

<!-- # maintainer responsibilities --------------------------------------- # -->

## Maintainer Responsibilities

Project maintainers are responsible for helping keep project spaces
productive, respectful, and welcoming.\
Maintainers may:

- Clarify or enforce this **Code of Conduct**.
- Ask participants to change inappropriate behavior.
- Moderate discussions when necessary.
- Remove content that violates project rules.
- Close discussions that become unproductive or abusive.
- Restrict participation when repeated violations occur.
- Take additional action when behavior poses a serious risk to
  the community.

Maintainers should make reasonable efforts to handle these situations
fairly and consistently.\
Maintainers are not expected to tolerate abusive behavior simply because
it occurs during a technical discussion.

<!-- # reporting --------------------------------------------------------- # -->

## Reporting a Problem

If you experience or witness behavior that violates this
**Code of Conduct**, please report it privately.\
For sensitive or serious matters, please contact:
[**`baginskioss@proton.me`**][email].

Please do not open a public GitHub Issue for Code of Conduct reports.\
When reporting an incident, please provide as much relevant information
as you are comfortable sharing, including:

- What happened.
- When and where it happened.
- Who was involved.
- Links to relevant discussions, issues, pull requests, or messages.
- Any additional context that may help understand the situation.

Please avoid sharing sensitive personal information publicly.\
If you are unsure whether something is a violation, you are still
welcome to report it privately.

<!-- # handling reports -------------------------------------------------- # -->

## Handling Reports

Reports will be reviewed as carefully and fairly as possible.\
Depending on the situation, the response may include:

1. Reviewing the report and available context.
2. Asking for additional information when necessary.
3. Speaking privately with the people involved.
4. Determining whether the behavior violates this Code of Conduct.
5. Taking appropriate action to address the situation.
6. Following up when additional action is necessary.

The appropriate response will depend on the circumstances, severity,
frequency, and impact of the behavior.\
Information related to reports will be handled with discretion and
shared only when reasonably necessary to address the situation.

<!-- # consequences ------------------------------------------------------ # -->

## Consequences

Behavior that violates this **Code of Conduct** may result in
actions such as:

- A private warning.
- A request to stop or change inappropriate behavior.
- Removal of inappropriate content.
- Temporary restrictions on participation.
- Closure or removal of discussions, issues, or pull requests.
- Temporary or permanent removal from project spaces.
- Other actions considered appropriate based on the circumstances.

Serious violations may result in immediate restrictions without
a prior warning.\
Actions will be based on the circumstances rather than applied
automatically in every situation.

<!-- # good faith -------------------------------------------------------- # -->

## Good-Faith Participation

This **Code of Conduct** is intended to protect the community, not to
discourage legitimate criticism or technical discussion.

**Reports should be made in good faith.**

A person should not be punished simply for making a reasonable
complaint, reporting a concern, disagreeing with a decision, or
expressing a technical opinion respectfully.

At the same time, intentionally making false reports, manipulating
evidence, or using the Code of Conduct to harass another participant
may itself be considered inappropriate behavior.

<!-- # scope ------------------------------------------------------------- # -->

## Scope

This **Code of Conduct** applies to project spaces and interactions
associated with these projects, including when participating through:

- GitHub repositories.
- Issues and discussions.
- Pull requests.
- Code reviews.
- Community channels.
- Project-related events.
- Other spaces where participants are representing or interacting
  with the project.

The same expectations apply whether you are a maintainer, contributor,
collaborator, user, or community member.

community standards and adapted to the needs of these projects.

It is intended to provide clear expectations for respectful
collaboration while keeping the communication style practical
and aligned with an open-source environment.

## Final Note

**Open-source projects are built through collaboration.**

People will make mistakes. Code will have bugs. Ideas will sometimes
be rejected. Technical decisions will sometimes be questioned.
Projects will evolve in ways that not everyone agrees with.

**That is part of building software.**

What matters is that we can work through these situations with respect,
honesty, and a willingness to improve.\
Thank you for helping make these projects a place where people can
build, learn, contribute, and share their work with others.
